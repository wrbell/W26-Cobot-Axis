About the team course project
This semester, your team will get to design, build, and test a mechatronic system of your choosing from a list of project ideas.

Time is limited, so it is okay if your test shows that your design does not perform well. In fact, it is much more important that you use a good engineering design process and analysis and report it well than that your tested design operates successfully. Professionalism in your written and oral reporting of the project is paramount.

This project is structured with a few checkpoints for instructor approval:

Phase 1: Ideation and Scope (2 weeks)

The instructor will present seed ideas for you to choose from or come up with your own. The project must involve the sensing or actuation of a mechanical, electronic, hydraulic, pneumatic, thermal, or optical system incorporating a microcontroller.

You submit a written memorandum of your project idea and the scope of your project. The instructor responds with feedback to modify the idea and scope and a go/no go decision on advancing to the next phase.

Phase 2: Design and Preliminary Analysis (6 weeks)

Then, you design your idea on paper (or electronically) and perform the requisite engineering analyses to support your design. You should be able to do this without using computational engineering analysis such as CFD, FEM, and multiphysics solvers. Calculus, differential equations, linear algebra, and design reference tables should be enough. Using MATLAB is fine for an analytical numerical model.

You will be consulting online catalogs and specification sheets.

Submit a written memorandum of your preliminary design, budget, and analysis as your project update. Again, the instructor responds with feedback to modify (perhaps simplify) the design and a go/no decision on advancing to the next phase.

Phase 3: Build, and Additional Design/Analysis (3 weeks)

It is likely that some of the design and analysis will need to be revisited in this phase.

A progress update memorandum to the instructor is required during this phase.

Phase 4: Test and Reporting (3 weeks)

Document the final design, the analysis, the testing, the public reporting, and further recommendations in a final written report.

Present an oral presentation of your design project during the final exam time slot.

Ask and answer technical questions during the final exam oral presentation.
